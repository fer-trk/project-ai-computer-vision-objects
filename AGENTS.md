# Working agreement

- Build a portfolio proof of concept measuring brand exposure in videos. Read `docs/specs.md` and the active issue; consult `docs/assignment.md` for the original assignment. Do not load all documentation routinely.
- Respect all four assignment levels, an eight-business-day maximum and zero financial cost. Do not assume an extension, activate paid services or incur usage charges.
- Prioritize the user's judgment and learning. Before each implementation block, explain its purpose, give a short recommendation and ask for the user's approach or agreement. Wait for their answer. Previously approved mechanical steps can continue without repeated questions.
- Keep specifications marked as drafts until reviewed. Never silently choose brands, data sources, architecture, model, hosting, quality targets or scope changes. Document approved decisions briefly in the relevant issue.
- Build cumulatively toward expert delivery. Work on one issue at a time; do not add speculative abstractions, unrelated refactors or dependencies. Ask before delegating work.
- Explain concepts in Spanish at the user's level. All code comments and docstrings must be in English. Use concise professional English for branch names, commit subjects and PR titles; documentation and issues may be in Spanish.
- Use task-focused publication names without references to Codex or agents. Do not misrepresent authorship or suppress required notices or license attribution.
- Use `main` plus one short-lived task branch, such as `feat/video-analysis`. No permanent `develop`. Remove a branch after merge. Do not merge until the user has reviewed the evidence and agreed.
- Follow Conventional Commits: `type(scope): imperative description`, with optional scope and breaking-change marker. Types: feat, fix, docs, test, refactor, perf, build, ci, chore, revert. Validate every PR commit and the squash title through CI once configured; local hooks are supplementary.
- Read only relevant files, summarize command output, avoid repeated searches and keep responses short. Do not sacrifice necessary explanations, required checks or acceptance evidence to save tokens.
- Validate behavioral changes with relevant checks and record results. Never claim that a level, test, deployment or learning objective is complete without evidence. Instructions alone do not enforce repository protections.
- Never commit secrets, unlicensed media, raw datasets, user uploads, generated crops or large model weights. Record asset provenance and licenses; agree on safe distribution of required demonstration assets and model artifacts.
- Close each block with what changed, verification evidence, limitations and the next decision. Update the issue rather than duplicating its history across files.
- Keep the Kanban faithful to actual work: use the fourteen deliverables in docs/plan.md, record decisions and blockers in their issues, and update status/checklists only from evidence. Preserve stable issue IDs and links; use Orden for delivery sequence.
- Prioritize sponsor logos on clothing and paddles in the main report for each complete video; keep static signage/net exposure in a separate secondary section. Do not claim product success from easy static-logo results. Record sample-quality feedback and review candidate logo regions at native resolution.
